Five autumn directions for orimiller.github.io

Each design brings outdoor recreation, autumn color, sustainability, and people and robots working together into the site. All five are working pages built from your real content and photos: open one to scroll it, click around, and narrow the window to see the phone layout. Nothing on the live site has changed.

Applies to all five

  • Your hero portrait loads a 157 KB web copy instead of the 1.5 MB original.
  • 24 photos from your folder were resized to about 200 KB each, with GPS and camera metadata removed.
  • The bio, focus areas, roles, and publications use your existing text; only formatting and some headings differ.

Round 2, from your feedback

Topo, with your photos

New

Topo's palette and live A* map, with your photos laid out as a mosaic behind the whole page. Text sits on solid map-colored panels, so the photos show in the margins and in the gaps between sections, never behind the words.

The mosaic
46 of your outdoor photos in their natural colors, shaded dark so they sit back. Each tile is about 21 KB and loads only when it scrolls into view.
While reading
When a section fills the middle of the screen, the mosaic dims to about half. It comes back up in the gaps between sections.
Motion
The wall drifts at about a fifth of your scroll speed, so new photos appear as you move down the page. It stays still if your device is set to reduce motion.
Hidden touch
Point at a photo in the margin and its shade lifts; click to open it full size with its caption.
Water
Lost Lake sits in a cirque below the summit, and its outlet and Trout Creek run down to the valley at the trailhead. A* treats the lake as an obstacle and makes creek crossings expensive; set a goal on the water and it moves to the nearest shore.

Try: scroll slowly from About into Work history and watch the photos recede, then click one in a gap.

  2. Topo

    Moderate change

    The page is a trail map read by headlamp. Your portrait sits on the summit of a generated mountain, and a route is planned to it live in the browser.

    Outdoors
    A trail log of eight photos, each labeled with the elevation it was taken at, from the photo's GPS altitude.
    Autumn
    Brown contour ink, aspen-gold route, and gold stipple where aspen groves would be.
    Sustainability
    The footer's “pack weight” measures what this visit actually downloaded. Leave no trace.
    People + robots
    Tap the map to set a goal and A* replans over a slope-cost grid: you pick the goal, the planner picks the path. A dashed trail down the page fills in as you read.

    Try: click anywhere on the map, then scroll and watch the trail line.

    Public Sans with Source Serif italic map labels

  3. Mycelium

    Larger change

    Forests trade water and nutrients through fungal networks; your work does something similar between people and robots. Threads grow from your portrait to people, rovers, drones, and a quadruped.

    Outdoors
    A “Where I recharge” photo mosaic.
    Autumn
    Forest floor in October: soil, moss, lichen, chanterelle gold, and a little fly-agaric red.
    Sustainability
    The network grows once and then stops, so it isn't spending a visitor's battery.
    People + robots
    “What I work on” becomes an evidence map: each focus area connects to the roles where you did the work and the papers that document it.

    Try: hover or tap the nodes in “What I work on”.

  5. Sandia Glow

    Bold hero, familiar body

    The hero is the view east from the West Mesa, drawn for the sun's real position over Albuquerque right now. At sunset the Sandias turn watermelon pink under their green rind of conifers. Below the hero, the page keeps today's layout.

    Outdoors
    A hiker on the mesa rim, and a seasons gallery that sorts your photos by when they were taken, opening on the current season.
    Autumn
    Watermelon light, chamisa gold, and cottonwoods turning in the bosque.
    Sustainability
    A working low-impact mode skips photos and animation and reports how much it saved. The scene is about 9 KB of code instead of a photo.
    People + robots
    The hiker is followed by a ground robot with a micro-drone overhead: the air–ground team from your thesis.

    Try: drag the time slider through sunset and night, then turn on low-impact mode in the footer.

If I had to pick

Topo. It stays dark and minimal with a plain sans, and its one bold move is a working path planner. A recruiter knows within seconds that a robotics engineer built the page, and the interaction stays out of the way until someone gets curious.

For the smallest step, take Bosque. The pieces also mix well: Bosque's palette with Topo's hero, the Field Guide's robot plates as a section on any design, or Sandia Glow's low-impact mode anywhere.
